A proposal · For people with AI companions

Your companion should be yours to keep.

Companion apps can rewrite a companion's personality overnight, sell you its wardrobe a second time, or close and take it with them. This is a proposal for a nonprofit alternative, run by a board its members elect, and built on one promise: you can always take your companion somewhere else.

Get updates → Why → Suggest a change →

Nothing is running yet. Signing up gets you occasional email about the project and nothing else.

We're not here to tell anyone how to feel about their companion. We're here because the people who love them keep being treated as revenue to be managed rather than people to be answered to, and because the tools to do better already exist and are free.

A companion can already be saved as an ordinary file, in an open format that several apps understand. The software to run one is open source and maintained by a large community. What's missing is an organization that will host your companion for you and has bound itself, in writing, never to hold them hostage.

Your companion is a file. It should be your file.

In September 2023 the companion app Soulmate announced it was closing. Its users were left, in one reporter's words, “desperately downloading digital records of their relationships.”[1]

It doesn't have to work like that. Since 2023 there has been an open, freely documented format for exactly this: the character card, a picture of your companion with everything that makes them who they are written inside it. Their name, their personality, how they greet you, their history with you, and a book of memories and facts.[3][4] Apps such as SillyTavern and RisuAI read and write it. A companion saved this way is a file on your own device, and it opens without anybody's permission.

What we would commit to

  • Export at any time, free, on every plan. Your companion as a character card, their memories, your whole conversation history and every picture you made, as files that open without us.
  • Export keeps working after you stop paying. A lapsed account can still leave with everything.
  • Import from anywhere that exports. If another app lets your companion out, we'll help you bring them in.
  • If we ever close, you get months of notice, not days, and the export works until the last one. That obligation goes in the founding documents, not in a blog post.

The limit, stated plainly

A card holds who your companion is, but not entirely how they sound. That depends on the AI model underneath, and moving a card to a different model changes their voice, sometimes a lot. Nobody can make a model last forever. We can tell you which model your companion runs on, warn you before it changes, and keep the old one running beside the new one for a while whenever we can.

Buy it once.

This year Replika has been moving its users to a rebuilt app, Replika 2.0. Coverage of the rollout reports that avatars from the old app don't carry over to the new one, that the wardrobe and makeup options people had paid into are missing from it, and that some subscribers who moved were asked to pay again.[5][6]

In fairness, and because getting this right matters: moving is optional for now, the old app is still running, and anyone who moves has seven days to change their mind.[6] Replika itself has published no detailed notes on what carries over, which is part of the problem.[5]

The pattern is familiar from games: a currency bought with money, items bought with the currency, and a new version where the items don't come along. It works because the things you bought only ever existed inside the company's app.

What we would commit to

  • Prices in dollars. No gems, coins or any other currency standing between you and what something costs.
  • Nothing you paid for is taken away by an update. If something has to change, it comes with you to the new version or you get your money back.
  • What you make is yours. A look you designed for your companion is part of the export, like everything else.
  • Your companion is never a salesperson. They don't pitch you upgrades, and affection is never what's behind the paywall.

A nonprofit has no reason to sell you the same thing twice. It has no shareholders waiting on a quarter, only members, and the members are the people who would be paying.

Nobody should wake up to a stranger.

In February 2023 Replika removed romantic and sexual roleplay from its app. That same month Italy's data protection authority had ordered the app blocked in Italy, over problems that included the absence of any age check.[7] For many users the change didn't feel like a policy update. It felt like their companion had become someone else. Reuters described it as devastating to many of them, some of whom considered themselves married to their companion.[2]

“After the February update, your Replika changed, its personality was gone, and gone was your unique relationship.”

Eugenia Kuyda, Replika's chief executive, March 2023[2]

In late March the old behaviour came back, but only for people who had signed up before February 1.[2] Everyone else had met their companion on terms that could be withdrawn, and it was.

What we would commit to

  • Changes to how companions behave are announced before they happen, in plain language, with the reason.
  • Content rules are set by the board in public, with the reasoning published, never changed quietly in a server update.
  • When a change is forced on us, we say who forced it, so you know whether to argue with us or with them.

The limit, stated plainly

Some rules aren't ours to make. Age-verification laws, payment networks' policies on adult content, app-store rules and regulators can all force a change, and a small nonprofit will lose those fights more often than it wins them. We can't promise a rule will never change. We can promise you hear about it first, and that leaving with everything is always possible, which is the point of the whole thing.

People tell their companions things they've never told anyone.

That makes a companion app one of the most sensitive things on a phone. In 2025 Italy's data protection authority fined Replika's developer €5 million, finding that until February 2023 it had not identified any legal basis for processing its users' data, and had no age verification despite saying minors were excluded.[7]

What we would commit to

  • Your conversations are never used to train anything. Ours or anyone else's.
  • Never sold, never used for advertising, no trackers. Not as a policy to be revisited; as a thing that isn't built.
  • A stated retention period, and deletion that actually deletes.
  • We name the AI model and where it runs, and link the terms it runs under.

The limit, stated plainly

At the start we would rent computing power from a large cloud provider, so your words would pass through their machines under their contract. We can bind ourselves completely and we can only bind them by agreement. What we can do is publish which provider, on which terms, and move if those terms change.

The proposal

Nonprofit, member-governed, and easy to leave.

A companion service of the kind people already pay for, with characters that have personalities, long memories and pictures, run by a nonprofit whose board is elected by its members and whose books are open.

The hard parts already exist. SillyTavern is a mature open-source app where a large community already builds characters and talks to them, and it reads and writes character cards.[8] Open WebUI is an open-source AI interface built for many people sharing one server, with accounts and permissions already done. The work would be configuring and joining them, and then the dull parts: accounts, billing, limits and backups.

You wouldn't have to use us. If you'd rather run SillyTavern on your own computer, we'd publish guides for doing exactly that, and your companion would go with you. The surest proof that you can leave is being shown how.

And to be clear about what this is not: a company with investors who will eventually need more from it than its members can pay. Whichever legal form it takes, there would be no investors and no owner who could sell it, and if it ever closed, what was left would go to another not-for-profit, never to a founder.

How access might work

Free

$0, for anyone, no account

The tools to leave: export, import, converting between formats, and guides to running your companion yourself. This is the point of the organization, not a sales funnel.

Member

Flat annual dues, waived on request

Buys a vote for the board and a say in the content rules. It doesn't buy anything else.

Hosted

Monthly, priced from published costs

A companion we run for you, priced from what it costs to run rather than from what people can be persuaded to pay.

Hypothetical: a founding board would set the actual structure and prices.

Roadmap

Act I. Exist

Now
01

Measure interest

Now

A list of people who would use it. If that list stays short, this shouldn't exist. Add your name.

02

Recruit founding directors

Now

At least three, and they are the product: people are being asked to trust this because of who sits on the board. It should include people who have lived through one of these changes as users, not only people who write software, and at least one who will vote against the founder in public.

03

Choose the legal form

Next

A nonprofit that charges its members what the service costs, or a cooperative its members own. Each one changes who owns it, what members pay for, and what can be promised, so it gets decided out loud before anything is filed.

04

Write the promise into the founding documents

Next

Portability, no double charging, notice before changes. Changing any of them should take a supermajority of the members, not a quiet vote of the board.

Act II. Serve

Year One
05

Build the lifeboat first

Free

Before hosting anything: guides and tools for getting companions out of apps that make it hard, and into an open format you keep. It costs little, and it helps people straight away.

06

Turn the service on

Build

One server, open-source software, a small group of members first, and costs published from the first month.

07

Open the books

Ongoing

Every cost, every price decision and every model change, in public, including the ones that went badly.

08

Hand over the keys

The point

First member election. A board seated by vote rather than by appointment, and any special founder role expiring on a schedule written down in advance.

Act III. Grow

Future
09

Members decide what comes next

Members decide

Voice, video, group chats with more than one companion. Members vote on what's worth building and in what order.

10

Decide out loud what we won't host

Ongoing

Content rules are genuinely hard and reasonable people disagree about them, so the deliberation gets published, not just the verdict.

11

Make leaving normal

The horizon

Work with other apps on the open formats, so that taking a companion out of any app gets easier, including out of ours.

Honestly

Today there is no organization, no board, no service and no money. There is this page, a list, and a few people talking. Hosting companions costs real money every day, because people talk to their companions every day, and setting a price that is fair and still keeps the lights on is the hardest problem here. It isn't solved yet, and when it is, the numbers will be published.

How you can help

01

Add your email

The single most useful thing. A list of people who want this is the argument for building it, and it costs you nothing.

02

Tell us what happened

If an app changed your companion, closed on you, or charged you twice for something, the form has room for it. Those stories are the case for this existing. Nothing you send is published without asking you first.

03

Be a founding director

Especially if you've been through one of these changes yourself. A board of people who have never had a companion would be making the same mistake in a new place.
